Paper Number: MM10330
Title: A Novel Cost Driven Disk Scheduling Algorithm 
       for Multi-Priority Multimedia Objects
Authors: Ghandeharizadeh, Huang and Kamel

In this paper, the authors propose a single-queue disk scheduling
algorithm and claim that its performance is superior to multi-queue
schemes.

There are two key flaws in this paper.

1. Objective function:

   The authors claim that there are four design parameters:   priority,
   seek time, deadline, and cost.  Merely minimizing the total cost may not
   be the best way to formulate the cost-driven problem in this setting.
   The design goal should be to minimize the per-bit transfer cost, or

   min \sum (cost_i / (seek time_i + transfer time_i)) \times deadline_i(t)
   i \in I

   The deadline(t) function can be a step function that returns a one
   if task i's deadline is met, and a zero if the deadline is missed.
   A more sophisticated function can make the scheduling model more
   flexible and interesting.

2. Cost can be formulated considering priority and vice versa:

   The authors characterize all the other disk scheduling schemes
   as "multi-queue" schemes.  In their experiments, they
   assume these schemes pay attention only to priority, not to cost.
   Based on this assumption, their experimental results show that the
   one-queue policy achieves lower cost.

   This comparison, however, is unfair.  A "multi-queue" scheme can
   determine a task's priority based on its cost, and the performance 
   can be equally good.  As a matter of common sense, priority is 
   often determined by several criteria, of which cost is one.
