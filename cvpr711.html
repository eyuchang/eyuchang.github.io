Reviews for Paper #711: A Variational Bayes Mixture Model for Unsupervised Image Segmentation
Review #1:
Key Contribution: Applies variational methods for model selection in Gaussian mixture models, results then applied to image segmentation by color.
Comments to Author: The math here is fine, reasonably novel, and comprehensible to one well versed in the literature.

Hagai Attias also gave a variation solution for Gaussian mixture model selection two years ago; his formulation is more elegant and computationally more tractable but presumably also more approximate. Or is it?

The split and merge operations are technically correct but not appear to be well motivated in the sense that they simply constitute moves in a search and do not usefully predict the posterior probability of the new model. There may be a theorem to that effect in the cited paper by Richardson and Green but I do not remember it.

The main weakness is the empirical section. I have seen these images color-segmented before and the results in this paper are just about the same as everybody else''s. Moreover, there is no comparison with any other algoriithm, and no reporting of run-times or even time-evolution of the posterior.! In short, this is a very computationally expensive algorithm and there''s no way to decide whether it''s preferable to any other recent image segmentation or GMM model-selection method.

Review #2:
Key Contribution: A formulation of unsupervised image segmentation using variational
Bayes. The main contributions are (1) formulating image segmentation
so that it is tractable using variational Bayes, and (2) proposing a
new method for "split" proposals which assist with model selection.
Comments to Author: There are some interesting ideas here and the approach seems
promising. The method appears to use standard variational Bayes
assuming independent Gaussian mixtures at every pixel. I.e. no
spatial coherence -- spatial coherence is only enforced in
postprocessing. The main novel contribution appears to be the sampling
method for "split" proposals. I''m no expert on variational Bayes,
but if this is really new it should be given much more prominence and
explained more clearly. In particular, why is it an efficient approach
to select the column corresponding to the maximum eigenvalue?

The results are reasonable but not spectacular.

There are many typos, which make the paper difficult to read. For
example on the first page known-> unknown, prior-> priori, section->
selection. In the bibliography, Deppster-> Dempster.

In section 3, you need to clearly explain which equ! ations and
assumptions are part of the standard variational Bayes approach, and
which are specific to this implementation (e.g. pixel-wise
independence).

In section 4.2, the theorem quoted is the standard and very well-known
fact that real symmetric matrices can be diagonalized -- it''s
confusing to present this as a theorem.

Please quote the calculation speed somewhere.
