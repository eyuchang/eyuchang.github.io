Subject: Brief outline of 3 main research components

Delivery of this technology on a mass scale will not be possible
without significant advances in photonic integration akin to the
revolution in electronic integration in the 1960s. However, the
development of integrated photonic technologies cannot be done in a
vacuum but must be done in concert with other circuit technologies,
with knowledge of how this technology will interplay with the network
architecture and it’s functions, the computing architecture and the end
user applications. It is equally important that the impact of pervasive
gigabit photons on the applications themselves and the way scientists,
engineering, students and the general public will do their work and
live their lives in the future.
There are three main components to this project:
1. The Photonic Integration Component of this program will investigate
how to bring photonic circuit integration from its current infancy
equivalent to that of electronics in the 1950s to a level needed as a
pervasive network technology. This part of the project will have three
sub-groups. The first subgroup will deal with functional integration
issues and will consist of a cross-disciplinary team of researchers in
the photonics, controls dynamics and embedded systems areas. The second
sub-group will investigate how to integrate functions using a variety
of available and upcoming circuit technologies and the potential for 3D
integration. This effort will investigate the possibility of
integrating different kinds of semiconductor materials, such as silicon
for electronics and indium phosphide for optical and photonic devices
as well as new nanophotonic techniques. The circuit technologies that
will be employed are photonics, optoelectronics, micromechanical
systems and electronics. The associated packaging issues will also fall
under the second sub-area. This level of integration will bring
together researchers and students in VLSI, photonics, solid state,
materials, chemical engineering and mechanical engineering. The third
sub-area involves the choice of platform integration, what choices of
materials will be utilized to implement multi-circuit functions.
Heterogeneous systems and monolithic approaches will be studied
including InP and Si semiconductor and glass systems.
2. The Architectural Integration Component will operate under the
assumption that a pervasive Gigabit photonic interface technology
exists. This research sub-area will investigate how end-to-end
applications will take advantage of and be impacted by this new
technology. New pervasive Gigabit applications will be identified,
studied and the interfaces prototoyped. We will ask the question of
what science will be liberated that is currently blocked by not having
this type of networking infrastructure and how this infrastructure will
impact the how to deliver education to future generations.
3. The Dark Fiber Research Network/Laboratory is a unique facility that
will enhance the infrastructure for research,  education and community
outreach efforts. Four University of California campuses will be
connected through an all-optical switch core and the network will be
reconfigurable by researchers and students from web browsers at remote
locations. The Dark Fiber Research Network will infuse education with
the excitement of discovery by giving students and researchers access
to something that does not exist for this community today. This
infrastructure will create the necessary community to explore photonic
information technologies and bring the  physical layer research
directly to the researchers.
