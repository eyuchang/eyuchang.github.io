I am currently a PhD candidate in the Electrical Engineering Department at Stanford University, where I also received my MS in Computer Science in 1994. I will be finishing my PhD in the summer of 1999. My advisor is professor Hector Garcia-Molina.

My research interests are in Multimedia and Database Systems. My thesis architects a media data delivery system that minimizes initial latency for the playback of media data (i.e., video and audio) while maintaining high throughput. The architecture consists of two parts: server and client. At the server side, we propose intelligent data placement, data replication, memory management and disk scheduling policies to manage parallel disks. At the client side, we propose a disk and memory cache that buffers the variability of the data delivery and supports "intelligent" VCR-like operations, such as pause and instant replay.

In addition to my thesis work, I work on content-based image retrieval and data mining projects. Together with two other PhD students, Chen Li and James Wang, we have been developing a Replicated IMagE copy detector (RIME) for the world-wide web. In RIME we use selected wavelet coefficients to represent images; we devise a clustering/encoding scheme, which groups similar images into clusters and store each cluster in a sequential file to avoid the curse of dimensionality that is inevitible in conducting search in high dimensional spaces. As to the data mining work, I worked with Dr. Mei Hsu and Dr. Umesh Dayal at HP Labs last summer and am consulting NEC Multimedia Research Lab at San Jose on managing and classifying bookmarks. Some of the mining related work have been submitted to Sigmod99 and WWW8 conferences for publication.

In the past summer, I instructed Database System Principles at Stanford University. The course was taught by professor Jeff Ullman in the prior Winter quarter. While two thirds of the lectures in the summer were replay of Jeff's lectures, I conducted one third of the lectures. We used a new textbook written by professors Hector Garcia-Molina, Jeff Ullman, and Jeniffer Widom.

Before joined Stanford as a full-time PhD student in 1995, I worked for Consilium (now a part of Applied Materials), Digital Equipment Corporation (now Compaq), and SunSoft for ten years. Prior to my experience and education in Computer Science, I received my BS and MS degrees in Industrial Engineering from Tunghai University, Taiwan and University of California, Berkeley, in 1981 and 1985 respectively.

In addition to my technical work, I am very interested in literature and philosophy. I deem philosophy the foundation of science and irrational thinking complementary to conducting technical research. I write and publish essay and poetry in Chinese.
