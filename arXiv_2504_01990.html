Possible Uncredited Borrowing: Did Liu et al. (2025) Extensively Use Ideas from the MACI Framework Without Attribution?

This report assesses the degree of conceptual and structural overlap between my MACI framework (Multi-LLM Agent Collaborative Intelligence) and the March 31, 2025 arXiv:2504.01990 paper/book titled “Advances and Challenges in Foundation Agents” by Bang Liu et al., which does not cite any of my MACI works.

Overview of Findings

Core Observation: There is significant thematic and technical overlap across multiple key components of MACI and the Liu et al. paper. While some ideas may stem from convergent evolution in AI research, the depth and specificity of the similarities—especially in structure, terminology, and methodology—strongly suggest uncredited borrowing.

Missing Citations Despite Public Visibility

The Liu et al. paper does not cite any of my prior published works, including:

  • SocraSynth (2023–2024)
  • CRIT (IEEE, 2023)
  • EVINCE (2024)
  • BEAM (IEEE MIPR 2024)
  • The Path to AGI textbook (SocraSynth.com, 2024)
Many of these have been widely read, indexed, and downloaded across platforms like ResearchGate, arXiv and IEEE Xplore, totaling over 20,000 times.

Was ChatGPT Used to Reconstruct MACI Work?

The Liu et al. paper exhibits linguistic markers often associated with AI-generated content: consistent tone, structured lists, and expansive yet vague descriptions. It is plausible that the authors used ChatGPT or similar tools to assist in drafting or ideating the paper.

If they prompted the model with questions like:

“Design a framework for multi-agent LLMs involving debate, reasoning validation, emotion regulation, and long-term planning.”
then ChatGPT may have drawn on concepts loosely resembling MACI, especially if fragments of similar public content were present in its training data or inferred through style.

Since the MACI textbook was open-sourced in 2024, it is entirely possible that public-facing content from the textbook or related blog posts influenced the model's responses or were paraphrased during generation—especially if the authors used GPT-based tools extensively without verifying originality.

Assessment of Intent

Given the:

  • Depth of conceptual overlap,
  • Structural similarities across multiple pillars,
  • Use of nearly identical terminology and frameworks,
  • Complete lack of citation despite public availability of my work,
the likelihood of purely independent invention appears low.

Verdict: This constitutes a strong case of uncredited intellectual borrowing that warrants clarification and acknowledgment.
