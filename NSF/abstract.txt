
In this study we show a data placement technique 
that takes popularity and bitrates of videos into
consideration to dynamically migrate data on 
disk to minimize per-stream cost (and hence it
maximizes throughput given a fixed budget).
We perform our analysis and evaluation on a single
disk and assume that a video are encoded at a
constant bitrate. In Section~\ref{} we discuss
that extending our technique for managing data
migration on parallel disks and for videos
encoded in variable bitrates can be straightfoward.

There are three key challenges we address in this paper. 
\begin{itemize}
\item How to determine the optimal layout of the videos
given that the bitrates and the popularity distribution of
the videos on the disk are known.
\item The bandwidth allocated for data migration should
not decrease the performance of the disk.
\item The frequent migration of the data should not
cause disk fragmentation.
\end{itemize}

is not constant. Since the recording density is
unform across the disk surface, the outer
tracks are longer than the inner ones, and the
disk rotates at a constant speed, the outetracks enjoys 
a higher data transfer rate than the inner ones.
