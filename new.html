I am currently a PhD candidate in the Electrical Engineering Department at Stanford University, where I also received my MS in Computer Science in 1994. I will be finishing my PhD in the summer of 1999. My advisor is professor Hector Garcia-Molina.

My research interests are in Multimedia and Database Systems. My thesis architects a media data delivery system that minimizes initial latency for the playback and fastscan of media data (i.e., video and audio) while maintaining high throughput. The architecture consists of two parts: server and client. At the server side, my thesis proposes intelligent data placement, data replication, memory management and disk scheduling policies to manage parallel disks. At the client side, my thesis proposes a disk and memory integrated cache that buffers the variability of the data delivery and supports "intelligent" VCR-like operations, such as pause and instant replay. Please see my thesis outline for details.

In addition to my thesis work, I work on content-based image retrieval and data mining projects. Together with two other PhD students, Chen Li and James Wang, we have been developing a copy detector, RIME, for finding unlawful copies of images on the world-wide web. In RIME we use selected wavelet coefficients to represent images; we devise a clustering/encoding scheme, which groups similar images into clusters and store each cluster in a sequential file to avoid the curse of dimensionality that is inevitable in conducting search in high dimensional spaces. As to the data mining work, I worked with Dr. Mei Hsu and Dr. Umesh Dayal at HP Research Labs last summer and am consulting NEC Multimedia Research Lab at San Jose on managing and classifying web pages. Some of the mining work have been submitted to conferences for publication.

In the past summer, I instructed Database System Principles at Stanford University. The course was taught by professor Jeff Ullman in the prior Winter quarter. While two thirds of the lectures in the summer were replay of Jeff's lectures from tapes, I conducted one third of the lectures. This teaching experience is valuable since it gives me opportunities to learn how to organize course materials, administrate homework and exams, and interact with students with diversified background. I received good evaluation from the students.

Before joined Stanford as a full-time PhD student in 1995, I worked for three companies, Consilium (now a part of Applied Materials), Digital Equipment Corporation (now Compaq), and SunSoft for ten years. Prior to my experience and education in Computer Science, I received my BS and MS degrees in Industrial Engineering from Tunghai University in Taiwan and University of California at Berkeley in 1981 and 1985, respectively.

Finally, besides my technical work, I am very interested in philosophy and literature. I have taken/audited more than ten courses in the School of Humanity at Stanford. I have no quarrel with Plato that rational thinking is the foundation of science, but I also believe in that irrational thinking may stretch the realms of the known rational boundary and help fostering visions for future technical research. I write and publish essay and poetry in Chinese.
